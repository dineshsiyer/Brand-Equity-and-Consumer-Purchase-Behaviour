# Market Research Project: Brand Equity and Consumer Purchase Behavior

This repository contains the project materials, analysis, and survey questionnaire used in a market research study examining the impact of brand equity on consumer purchase behavior. Data was collected from over 300 respondents via a Google Form survey and analyzed using SPSS.

## Introduction

Understanding the relationship between brand equity and consumer purchase behavior is essential for businesses aiming to enhance brand loyalty and improve market positioning. This project investigates how different dimensions of brand equity—such as awareness, perceived quality, and emotional connection—influence consumers’ decisions to purchase a product.

## Objectives

- **Primary Objective:** To understand the impact of brand equity on consumer purchase behavior.
- **Secondary Goals:**
  - Assess the role of brand awareness and loyalty.
  - Examine the influence of perceived quality and value on purchase decisions.
  - Analyze demographic factors (e.g., gender, age, location, qualification, and income) that could affect consumer behavior.

## Data Collection

Data was collected using an online questionnaire distributed via Google Forms. The survey reached a diverse audience through social media channels, employing a convenience sampling method. In total, responses from more than 300 participants were recorded.

### Questionnaire Overview

The questionnaire is divided into two sections:

1. **Responder’s Information:** Questions about gender, age, household income, education level, and hometown.
2. **Brand Perception:** A series of 30 items measured on a 5-point Likert scale (with 5 = Strongly Disagree and 1 = Strongly Agree) that assess various aspects of brand equity, including brand loyalty, trust, product recommendation, and the influence of advertising.

For full details, refer to the [Questionnaire.pdf](./Questionnaire.pdf) included in this repository.

## Methodology

The analysis was conducted using SPSS with the following tests performed:

- **Reliability Test:** Significance Value = 0.860.
- **Validity Test:** Significance Value = 0.000.
- **Normality Test:**  
  - Male: p = 0.007  
  - Female: p = 0.082.
- **Homogeneity Test:** Significance Value = 0.853.
- **Mann-Whitney Test (Gender):** p = 0.025.
- **Kruskal-Wallis Test:**  
  - Age: p = 0.000  
  - Qualification: p = 0.000  
  - Location: p = 0.065  
  - Income: p = 0.002.

These tests validate the survey's structure and reveal significant differences and correlations between demographic variables and brand perception.

## Data Analysis

The SPSS analysis provided several key insights:

- **Gender:** Differences in brand perception were significant between male and female respondents.
- **Age:** Younger consumers showed a stronger preference for established brands.
- **Education and Income:** Higher levels of education and income were associated with stronger brand loyalty and preference.
- **Location:** Geographic location had minimal impact on brand preference, suggesting that brand loyalty transcends regional boundaries.

Detailed analysis can be found in the [Findings.pdf](./Findings.pdf).

## Managerial Implications

The insights drawn from this study offer actionable recommendations for brand managers:

- **Targeted Marketing:** Tailor marketing strategies to address differences in gender and age demographics.
- **Enhancing Brand Loyalty:** Invest in initiatives that build and maintain brand loyalty, focusing on quality assurance and consistent brand messaging.
- **Segmentation Strategies:** Develop differentiated campaigns for higher-income and higher-education segments.
- **Further Research:** Although location was not significant, continued monitoring of regional trends is advisable.


## Future Work
